A heading can be larger than the text below it without competing with every other element. Choose a clear starting point, then use smaller changes in size to show supporting levels.

Start with one clear emphasis

Compare sizes together, including the heading, body text and image. A useful hierarchy depends on their relationship rather than on one element alone.

Judge a heading beside its paragraph and image rather than in isolation. The relationship between elements carries more information than a single font size chosen on its own.

Keep the number of size changes modest. When every label demands attention, readers have to decide which one matters before they can begin reading the content.
